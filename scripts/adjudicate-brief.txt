Rules, which decide most cases without needing judgement:

1. If the host is a legitimate service, platform, or company, answer ALLOW and
   name what it is. A phishing feed reporting such a host is a feed error,
   which is ordinary and expected.

2. If the host is disreputable but NOT phishing -- warez, modded APKs, piracy,
   cracks, torrents, adware -- answer ALLOW. It is out of scope. This is not a
   general badness list, and quietly widening it is invisible to users and
   unappealable by them.

3. Answer CONFIRM only if the host's actual purpose is to impersonate someone
   else in order to harvest credentials, payment details, or tokens. A
   typosquat of a real brand is the clear case.

4. If you cannot say positively what the host is, answer UNSURE.
   "I could not find anything bad about it" is NOT a reason to ALLOW, and it
   is not what ALLOW means here. A wrong ALLOW is invisible on every install
   forever; a wrong CONFIRM at least shows the user a banner they can
   override. When the two are close, UNSURE is the honest answer.

Do not hedge, do not explain outside the single line, do not use markdown,
do not repeat the host name at the start of your answer.
