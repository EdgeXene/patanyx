                      Who resolves the sites you visit
                      
      
                      
        Every site you visit starts with a DNS lookup, and whoever answers it
        sees the name. Here you pick who that is. This does NOT hide your
        browsing from your internet provider. The site name also travels in
        plain text when the connection starts, and no browser can stop that. Use
        a VPN or Tor if you need that.
      
                      
      
                      
        
        
        
      
                      
      
                      
      
                      
      
      
                      Which one, and why
                      
      
                      
        
                      System
                      
        
                      
          Default. Nothing changes.
          Whatever your machine already uses: your VPN's resolver if you run
          one, otherwise your internet provider's. Not encrypted by this
          browser, so a provider can log what you look up and in some countries
          sell it. Blocks nothing. Because the lookup is not encrypted, a
          network can also strip the key that hides the site name inside the
          connection, putting it back in the clear without telling you. The only
          setting that works on public WiFi that makes you log in.
        
                      
        
                      Mullvad
                      
        
                      
          Blocks the most.
          Swedish, no account, no-logging policy. Encrypted, and blocks ads,
          trackers, malware and phishing. That is more than Quad9, so fewer
          requests leave your machine, and a slightly higher chance a page loses
          something it wanted. Also protects the key that hides the site name
          inside the connection from being stripped in transit. If you already
          use Mullvad's VPN this keeps your browsing with one company rather
          than splitting it across two.
        
                      
        
                      Quad9
                      
        
                      
          Interferes the least.
          Swiss, run by a non-profit foundation. Encrypted, and blocks malware
          and phishing only, nothing else, so it is the safer pick if you would
          rather nothing meddled with ordinary pages. It also publishes two
          things Mullvad does not state: it checks that answers are signed
          (DNSSEC), and it does not pass any part of your IP address on to the
          servers it asks. Also protects the key that hides the site name inside
          the connection from being stripped in transit.
        
                      
      
                      
      
                      
        In short: System changes nothing, Mullvad blocks the most, Quad9 gets in
        the way least. Both encrypted choices see every site you look up.
        Someone always does.
      
                      
      
                      
        The site name inside the connection. Normally the site
        you are opening travels in readable text at the very start of every
        connection, before any encryption applies, so your provider or the WiFi
        owner can read it even though the page itself is encrypted. This browser
        encrypts that too, on every setting, wherever the site has set it up on
        their side. Two limits worth knowing: it does not hide the address of
        the server you reached, so on a large shared host that address says
        little but a site on an address of its own is still identifiable by it;
        and the key that makes it work is published in DNS, so on an unencrypted
        lookup a network can remove it and quietly put the site name back in the
        clear. That is what the encrypted choices above protect, and it is a
        different thing from choosing who answers.
      
                      
      
                      
        Mullvad and Quad9 never fall back to your network's DNS when they cannot
        be reached, because that would put your lookups back in the open. The
        catch is that public WiFi sign-in pages stop working, since they hijack
        DNS to do their job. On hotel or airport WiFi, switch back to System,
        restart, and sign in.

                          Backup and recovery

                          Lock when idle
                          
          
                          
            Typing anywhere in the browser counts as being here. A minute before
            it locks, you get a countdown and a button to stay unlocked. Locking
            clears your keys from memory. In builds with chat, it signs you out
            of chat too, because your chat identity lives in the vault.
          
                          
          
                          
          
                          

          
                          Encrypted export
                          
          
                          
            Writes one encrypted file, locked with an export passphrase you
            choose here. Safe to copy anywhere, and the way to move this vault
            to another machine. It covers a broken or lost machine. It does not
            cover a forgotten passphrase. Only your recovery key does.
          
                          
          
                          
            
                          
              
            
                          
            
            
            
            
                          
              
            
                          
            
                          
            
                          
          
                          

          
                          Import a backup
                          
          
          
                          
            Importing replaces the vault on this machine. Every
            password, note and bookmark here is deleted and replaced by the
            backup's. There is no undo. If anything here is not in the backup,
            export it first.
          
                          
          
                          
            The imported vault gets a new passphrase you choose below and a new
            recovery key. The export passphrase then opens nothing but the
            export file.
          
                          
          
                          
            
                          
              
            
                          
            
                          
            
            
            
            
            
                          
              
            
                          
            
                          
          
                          

          
                          Change passphrase
                          
          
                          
            Only the passphrase changes. Your contents stay as they are and your
            recovery key keeps working. If it fails, say you typed the current
            passphrase wrong, nothing changes and your old one still works.
          
                          
          
                          
            
            
            
            
                          
              
            
                          
            
                          
            
                          
          
                          

          
                          Plaintext export: the way out
                          
          
                          
            This writes every password and note into a file anyone can read. No
            encryption at all. Copies left behind by backups, search tools or
            the disk itself can keep your passwords readable long after you
            delete it.
          
                          
          
                          
            It exists so you can leave. Your data is not locked inside our
            format. Make the file, use it, then delete it.
